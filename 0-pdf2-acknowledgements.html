Acknowledgements

Completing this PhD dissertation would not have been possible without the guidance, support, and encouragement of many people. I would therefore like to express my sincere gratitude to everyone who contributed to this work and supported me throughout my PhD journey.

First and foremost, I would like to thank my main supervisor, Adam Hulman. Your guidance and support have been invaluable throughout the past three years. Your creative ideas and extensive knowledge of both epidemiology and data science have strengthened this project and equipped me with valuable skills that I will carry into the next stage of my career. Thank you for giving me this opportunity, for believing in me, and for securing the funding that made part of this PhD possible.

I am also deeply grateful to the other members of my supervisory team. When changes in my original supervisory team disrupted the initial plans for the project during the first month of my PhD, Anders Aasted Isaksen, Guy Fagherazzi, and Signe Toft Andersen generously stepped in to help guide the project forward. Anders, I am sincerely grateful for the time, effort, and support you contributed to the scoping review. Your exceptional technical abilities, combined with your clinical expertise, have made you an important source of advice and inspiration. I am equally grateful for the welcoming and accepting atmosphere you helped create in the research environment at Steno Diabetes Center Aarhus and your awesome quarto template for the PhD dissertation. Guy, thank you for your insightful comments and for welcoming me into your research group during my stay at the Luxembourg Institute of Health. I am particularly grateful for the opportunity to meet and work alongside your wonderful team, participate in the team retreat, and feel so genuinely included throughout my visit. Signe, thank you for giving me the opportunity to work with the CANCAN data after access to the originally planned data was lost. Your clinical expertise, thorough explanations, and consistently positive attitude have been both encouraging and inspiring. Our discussions have been among the most enjoyable and eye-opening parts of my PhD.

I would also like to thank Ole Nørgaard and Tue Helms Andersen for their expertise and assistance in developing the literature-search strategy. Your guidance greatly improved the quality of this dissertation and taught me how to conduct literature searches in a more systematic and rigorous manner. Thank you to Benjamin Lebiecka-Johansen and Livie Yumeng Li for their technical assistance with GenomeDK, and to William P. T. M. van Doorn for providing coding support and generously offering help when needed. Thank you to Sanne Fisker for allowing me to accompany her in the clinic and for showing me how the Steno T1D Risk Engine can be used in conversations with patients. Observing these consultations gave me valuable insight into the complex and challenging task of supporting people with diabetes in making decisions about their health. I am also grateful to Tibor V. Varga for welcoming me to visit his research group at the University of Copenhagen and for sharing his expertise in algorithmic fairness.

I feel fortunate to have met so many wonderful people at Steno Diabetes Center Aarhus (SDCA) and through the Graduate School of Health at Aarhus University. You made me feel welcome, and I never felt isolated or alone during this project. Through the PhD Association at Health, I had the opportunity to meet fellow PhD students from many different research fields, exchange experiences, and find support in navigating the practical and administrative aspects of PhD life. I am equally grateful to my colleagues and office mates at SDCA, who ensured that there was always someone with whom to share a conversation or a lunch break. Livie, Nina, Manu, Jonas, Jie, Thien, and many others. Your company has meant more to me than you may realize.

I would also like to express my sincere gratitude to the Danish Diabetes and Endocrine Academy and the Danish Cardiovascular Academy for funding this PhD project, believing in its potential, and providing valuable courses and opportunities to develop professional networks. The communities created by the academies have enriched my PhD experience and made this project possible. I am also grateful to Julie and Inger for always responding quickly and patiently whenever the administrative aspects of travel and other practical matters became confusing.

Finally, I would like to thank my friends and family for supporting me beyond my academic work. Getting an eight-week-old puppy in the middle of a PhD may have been a slightly ambitious decision, but it all worked out, with a great deal of help. Above all, thank you to my parents for the many hours of puppy-sitting and for always stepping in when I needed to travel or when work became particularly demanding. I am also deeply grateful to Astrid, Anna, Livie, Nina, Yasmina and Daniel for bringing food, looking after my puppy, giving feedback and helping me whenever I needed it. Thank you all for being there for me and for supporting me throughout this journey. Lastly, thank you to my dog Bonnie for bringing joy and balance to my life and for always making even the most difficult working days a little brighter.
